# The Fog Doesn't Take Names

Every night, the fog comes into our town. And it takes anything nobody's saying the name of.

That's why we name everything. The lampposts are Gus, Gus Two and Linda. Mr. Pell named his sandwich Kevin so it'd still be there for lunch.

Once, someone forgot to say "mailbox." Now there's a hole where the mailbox used to be, and letters just fall in it.

I'm the Caller. Every night at midnight, I stand on a crate in the square and say every name in town, out loud, in order. 212 names. Every person, every house, every dog. Took me two years to memorize.

As long as I'm talking, the fog stays out.

Last Tuesday, I got a cold.

By name 80, my voice cracked. By name 120, it was a whisper. The fog rolled past the fence. The bakery sign went blank. Then the bakery.

By name 150, I had no voice at all.

Gus went out. Then Gus Two. Mrs. Harlow's porch vanished while she was standing on it. She stepped back inside just in time.

The fog was one street from the square. It moved one house at a time, quiet, like it was reading a list.

I couldn't say the names. But I knew who could.

I ran to Mr. Pell's door and banged on it. He opened it. I pointed at the house next door.

"...Doris?"

Doris's house snapped back.

I ran door to door. Point. Point. Point. Doris said Ray. Ray said the twins. The twins screamed their dog's name so loud the fog actually backed up.

Then the whole town was out on their porches, shouting names at each other. And the fog slid all the way back to the fence.

Everyone cheered. I sat down on my crate.

That's when I felt it. Cold. Around my feet.

I say everyone's name every night. Nobody's ever said mine.

My legs started to fade. People turned to look. Nobody spoke. Nobody knew it.

Then my little sister pushed through the crowd.

"...Theo?"

The fog snapped away like it got burned.

Now every night at midnight, I say 212 names.

"THEO!"

And they say one.
