# Twinkletoes

In this world, every time you get embarrassed, you shrink one inch.

Trip in the hallway? An inch. Wave back at someone who wasn't waving at you? Another inch. And whoever embarrassed you, they get that inch. They grow.

So everyone here is very, very careful. Nobody tells jokes at weddings. Karaoke is basically illegal. And my cousin's voice cracked once during his graduation speech. They had to hand him his diploma with tweezers.

Then there's Mr. Crane. He runs the only TV station in town, and every Friday he hosts Gotcha!, a live show where he pranks people in front of everyone. He trips them. He reads their diaries out loud. He puts their baby pictures on a forty-foot screen.

He's nine feet tall. He has to duck under stoplights. And nobody has ever embarrassed Mr. Crane. Not once.

He makes everyone call him Mr. Crane. Nobody in town knows his first name. Except my mom.

My mom does the paychecks at the station. She's the only person who's ever seen his real name, and she made me swear never to say it.

Because my mom is also his favorite target. Every week he drags her on camera. Every week she comes home a little smaller. She's two feet tall now. She stands on a chair to reach the stove. Last week our cat tried to carry her off by the collar.

Then the doctor said one more inch, and she might get too small to find.

"Just don't let him get to you, Mom," I begged her. "Please." She promised.

Until the season finale. Gotcha! Live, in the town square. Every family in town was there.

And Mr. Crane pointed his giant finger right at my mom. "Look, folks! It's the incredible shrinking accountant!" Everybody laughed, because they were scared not to.

Then he held up her lunchbox, with the little note I'd drawn her inside. "Aww. Did your baby make you this?"

My mom's face turned bright red. I watched her start to sink.

"Mom, don't." I stepped right in front of her. I looked all the way up at Mr. Crane. And I grabbed the microphone.

"Hey, everybody. Did you know Mr. Crane's first name is Twinkletoes?"

The whole square went quiet. Mr. Crane's ears turned pink. "Who told you that?"

"It's on every paycheck. Twinkletoes Crane."

Bloop. He shrank an inch. And I felt it. My sneakers got tight. I got taller. It worked.

And that's when I realized it. Mr. Crane had embarrassed every single person in this square, which meant every single person here could embarrass him right back.

I turned to the crowd. "Twinkletoes!"

And they got it.

"Twinkletoes!" yelled the lunch lady he pushed into a vat of pudding. Bloop.

"Nice name, Twinkletoes!" yelled the mailman he dressed up as a baby on live TV. Bloop, bloop.

"Twinkle! Toes! Twinkle! Toes!" I was the loudest one there. Bloop, bloop, bloop.

All around me, people were growing. Grandmas got their inches back. Little kids popped up like popcorn. And Mr. Crane was shrinking. Eight feet. Six. Four.

Then my mom climbed onto the stage, took the microphone, and said it herself. "Goodnight, Twinkletoes."

Bloop. And again. Bloop, bloop, bloop, bloop.

She grew until she was looking down at him. And standing where Mr. Crane used to be was the tiniest man I've ever seen. Too small for the cameras to find. Too small to embarrass anybody, ever again.

He scurried off the stage and down a storm drain, squeaking the whole way.

My mom was five foot six again. She tried to pick me up and spin me around, but she couldn't. Probably because I'm seven feet tall now. I'm ten. My bed's too short, none of my clothes fit, and I can't fit on the school bus.

Worth it.
