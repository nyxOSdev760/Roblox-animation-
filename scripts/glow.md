# Glow

In this world, every time someone ignores you, you fade. Just a little. A little more see-through.

Wave at someone and they don't wave back? Fade. Text the group chat and nobody answers? Fade. And whoever ignored you, they get brighter. They glow.

So everyone here is very, very polite. Teachers learn every single name. Leaving someone on read is basically a crime. And my uncle told a joke at Thanksgiving once and nobody laughed. We had to find him with a flashlight.

Then there's Brayden. He's the most popular kid in school, and he glows like a neon sign. Because his whole thing is ignoring people. He walks past you in the hallway like you're air. He leaves everyone on read. He sits alone at lunch on purpose, so you have to come to him.

He's so bright you can't look straight at him. Planes use him to land at night. And nobody has ever ignored Brayden. Not once.

Because everybody wants Brayden to notice them. Especially my little sister, Lily.

Lily's in his grade. She says hi to him every single morning. And every single morning, he looks right through her. She's so faded now you can see the fridge through her. The dog walks into her. Last week the teacher marked her absent while she was sitting in the front row.

Then the nurse said, if she fades much more, we might not get her back.

"Just stop saying hi to him," I begged her. "Please." She promised.

Until the school talent show. The whole school was there. Every parent too.

And Lily got up on stage to sing. Halfway through her song, Brayden stood up in the front row, put on his sunglasses, and walked out. Everybody saw it.

Lily's microphone dropped. And I watched her start to disappear.

"Lily, don't." I ran onto the stage. I grabbed the microphone. And I looked right at Brayden in the doorway.

Then I turned my back on him.

"Hey everybody," I said. "Who wants to hear Lily finish her song?"

The whole gym went quiet. Brayden said, "Uh, hello?"

I didn't look at him. Nobody did.

Flicker. He dimmed. Just a little. And I felt it. I got brighter. My hands were glowing. It worked.

And that's when I realized it. Brayden had ignored every single person in this gym, which meant every single person here could ignore him right back.

"Hey," Brayden said. "I'm right here."

The lunch lady he walked past for three years turned her back. Flicker.

The kid he left on read four hundred times turned his back. Flicker, flicker.

The whole basketball team, the whole band, every single teacher turned around. Flicker, flicker, flicker.

All around me, people were lighting up. Kids who'd been see-through for years came back in full color. And Brayden was going dark. Brighter than the sun. Then a lamp. Then a nightlight.

Then Lily picked up the microphone and finished her song. And she didn't look at him once.

Flicker. And again. Flicker, flicker, flicker, flicker.

She glowed until the whole gym was gold. And where Brayden used to be, there was just a faint, flickery outline. Too dim to see. Too dim to ignore anybody ever again.

He walked out the door and nobody noticed. Not even the door.

Lily was solid again. She hugged me so hard she almost broke a rib. Then she let go and squinted. Probably because I'm glowing now. Like, a lot. I can't sleep without a blanket over my head, bugs follow me everywhere, and a plane tried to land on our roof.

Worth it.
