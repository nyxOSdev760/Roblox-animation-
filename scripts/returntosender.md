# Return to Sender

In this town, whatever you throw away comes back to you by morning. Same spot. Same smell.

So nobody throws anything away. Ever. Garbage trucks are a myth. My dentist keeps every tooth he's ever pulled in a drawer labeled "Regrets." And my uncle tried to throw out a banana peel in 1998. It's now in the town museum, and it's still warm.

Everyone's houses are full. My family sleeps in the bathtub because of a couch we can't get rid of.

Then there's Mayor Crumb. He has never had a single thing come back to him. Because he found a loophole.

He doesn't throw things away. He gives them away.

"Congratulations! A free sofa!" "A gift! One broken piano!" "Happy birthday! Here's a fridge. It's empty. It's also on fire."

And you can't throw away a gift. It's the rule. So every house in town is slowly filling with Mayor Crumb's junk.

Then he gave my grandma a boat.

An actual boat. She lives on the second floor. It came through the wall. She's now sleeping in the boat. In her own living room. Because the house is mostly boat.

The doctor said one more gift and the house would collapse with her in it.

So at the town festival, I walked up while he was handing out a four-ton statue of himself. I smiled. "Mayor Crumb. I have a gift for YOU."

I gave him a thank-you card.

He laughed. "That's it?"

"Open it."

Inside was a list. Every single thing he'd ever given anyone. And a line at the bottom: "Return all gifts to sender."

The whole town cheered. Then every person in town walked to his house and handed it all back. The boat. The piano. The fridge. The banana peel.

"Thank you for the gift," everyone said, one at a time. Thunk. Thunk. Thunk.

He's still under there. We think. Nobody can get him out.

And the town made me mayor, because of what I did. The first job is that I have to keep everything. Including him.

He's in my guest room. I can't throw him away.
